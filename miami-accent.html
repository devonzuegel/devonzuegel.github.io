01

The sounds

Do these in order. The L alone gets you more than half the way.

The signature

The heavy L

LIH-te-ra-LEE · PUB-Lix · MiL-Lion

Every L is fully articulated, tongue pressed hard against the ridge, never softened or swallowed. Other Americans let the L fade; Miami turns it up. Say "literally" with three loud Ls and people will already ask where you're from.

Vowels

Spanish vowels

hahnd · mahn · goh · Mee-AH-mee

Pure, short, no glide. "Go" is a flat goh, not goh-oo. "Hand" and "man" open up toward ah. The city's own name is the easiest one to practice.

Rhythm

Machine-gun cadence

ta-ta-ta-ta-ta, not ta-TAAA-ta

Spanish is syllable-timed; so is Miami English. Give every syllable nearly the same length instead of stretching the stressed ones. Rhythm matters more than any single sound.

Consonants

Harder consonants

dat · wah-ter · bet-ter

"Th" in the/that leans toward d. No flapped T: "water" stays wah-ter, never wadder.

Word swaps

Supposably & friends

supposably · irregardless

Say them with total confidence. Never correct yourself.

03

Party tricks

Literal translations from Spanish that locals say without noticing. FIU's Phillip Carter documented these as the core of the dialect.

Get down from the car.
bajar del carro · not "get out of"
Make the line.
hacer la fila · stand in line
Make a party.
hacer una fiesta · throw a party
Throw a photo.
tirar una foto · take a photo
She's married with him.
casada con · married to
Thanks God.
gracias a Dios · thank God
I'm super hungry.
súper as the universal intensifier
Put me the light.
ponme la luz · turn on the light
Give me a chance.
dame chance · hold on / let me by
Meat empanada.
empanada de carne · used by immigrants and Miami-born alike
Eating shit.
comiendo mierda · wasting time, hanging out
He has 30 years.
tiene 30 años · he's 30
Drink a pill.
tomar una pastilla · take a pill

Bonus move: speak flat English, then say one Spanish name or word (Alejandro, Publix's croquetas) with a full accent switch for exactly one word, then snap back.

05

Drill plan

1

Shadow, 5 min a day. Ten-second clip, repeat instantly, mimic rhythm before sounds. Record and A/B.

2

Anchor sentence. Drill the one at the top until every L, vowel, and syllable lands without thinking.

3

Field recordings. Publix checkout, a ventanita, Uber drivers. Ask a Miami-born friend for voice memos of the calques.

4

Exaggerate, then dial back. Nail the TikTok version first because it's easy to hear. Soften 30% for real conversations.

5

One calque per day. Use it in a real sentence with someone who won't notice. If they don't react, it worked.
